SECTION 1 — DESIGN WIND SPEED AND PRESSURE
================================================================================

BASIC WIND PRESSURE (SIMPLIFIED FORMULA):
  q = 0.00256 × V²    [psf, V in mph]
  q = 0.613 × V²      [Pa, V in m/s]   ← use this for SI calculations

  Wind speed design values:
    50 mph (22.4 m/s): q = 0.613 × 22.4² = 308 Pa (6.4 psf)
    70 mph (31.3 m/s): q = 0.613 × 31.3² = 600 Pa (12.5 psf)   — operational max
    90 mph (40.2 m/s): q = 0.613 × 40.2² = 990 Pa (20.7 psf)   — survival design
    100 mph (44.7 m/s): q = 0.613 × 44.7² = 1225 Pa (25.6 psf) — coastal/severe

  FIELD DEPLOYMENT DEFAULT:
    Operational design: 70 mph (survival); 50 mph (operating with antenna)
    Elevated antenna installation ≥25 ft: 90 mph survival minimum

GUST FACTOR:
  EIA/TIA-222-H applies a gust factor G = 0.85 for mast heights < 30 ft.
  This specification uses G = 1.0 (conservative; no gust reduction).

HEIGHT CORRECTION (EXPOSURE CATEGORY C — open terrain):
  K_z = (z/15)^(2/9.5)  where z = height in feet
  At 20 ft: K_z = (20/15)^0.211 = 1.06
  At 30 ft: K_z = (30/15)^0.211 = 1.16
  At 40 ft: K_z = (40/15)^0.211 = 1.24

  SIMPLIFIED: Use K_z = 1.2 for all heights in this specification (conservative).

DESIGN VELOCITY PRESSURE (adjusted):
  q_design = q × K_z × G = q × 1.2 × 1.0 = 1.2q

SECTION 2 — FORCE ON CYLINDRICAL MAST SECTIONS
================================================================================

DRAG FORCE ON CYLINDRICAL SECTION:
  F = q × Cd × A_projected

  Drag coefficient for circular cylinder:
    Slender cylinder (L/D > 20): Cd = 1.0
    Short cylinder (L/D < 5):    Cd = 0.8
    USE: Cd = 1.2 (includes 20% for surface roughness and interference)

  Projected area:
    A = D × L    [m², D = outer diameter, L = section length]

EXAMPLE — 50mm OD, 1.5m section:
  A = 0.050 × 1.5 = 0.075 m²
  F_section = q × 1.2 × 0.075
  At 70 mph (q = 600 Pa): F = 600 × 1.2 × 0.075 = 54 N per section

FULL MAST WIND LOAD (sum of all sections, 30-foot aluminum mast):
  Section 1 (38mm × 1.5m): F = 600×1.2×0.057 = 41 N  at h = 8.5m center
  Section 2 (44mm × 1.5m): F = 600×1.2×0.066 = 47 N  at h = 7.0m
  Section 3 (50mm × 1.5m): F = 600×1.2×0.075 = 54 N  at h = 5.5m
  Section 4 (57mm × 1.5m): F = 600×1.2×0.086 = 62 N  at h = 4.0m
  Section 5 (63mm × 1.5m): F = 600×1.2×0.095 = 68 N  at h = 2.5m

  Total mast drag: 272 N
  Point of action (resultant): ~5.5m above base

SECTION 4 — BASE MOMENT AND MAST BENDING STRESS
================================================================================

OVERTURNING MOMENT AT BASE (unguyed mast — cantilever model):
  M_base = Σ(F_section × h_section) + F_antenna × H_total

  30-FOOT UNGUYED EXAMPLE:
    M_mast = 41×8.5 + 47×7.0 + 54×5.5 + 62×4.0 + 68×2.5
           = 348 + 329 + 297 + 248 + 170
           = 1,392 N·m (from mast sections alone)

    Adding wire dipole at top (F = 80 N × 9m):
    M_antenna = 80 × 9.0 = 720 N·m

    M_total = 2,112 N·m (unguyed)

BENDING STRESS AT BASE SECTION (Section 5, 63mm OD × 3mm wall):
  Section modulus: S = π(OD⁴ - ID⁴) / (32 × OD/2)
    OD = 63mm, ID = 57mm
    I = π(63⁴ - 57⁴)/64 = π(15,752,961 - 10,556,001)/64 = 254,896 mm⁴
    S = I / (OD/2) = 254,896 / 31.5 = 8,092 mm³ = 8.09 × 10⁻⁶ m³

  σ_bending = M / S = 2,112 / (8.09 × 10⁻⁶) = 261 MPa

  FAILURE: 261 MPa exceeds 6061-T6 yield (276 MPa) — marginal unguyed!

  CONCLUSION: A 30-foot aluminum telescoping mast MUST be guyed.
  Even a single level of guying at 60% height reduces base moment by ~70%.

GUYED MAST MOMENT (first level at 5.4m, 60% of 9m):
  M_guyed = Σ(F × h) only for sections BELOW the guy level
  Section 3: F=54 N × 5.5m = 297 N·m
  Section 4: F=62 N × 4.0m = 248 N·m
  Section 5: F=68 N × 2.5m = 170 N·m
  M_guyed = 715 N·m (reduction of 66%)

  σ_guyed = 715 / 8.09×10⁻⁶ = 88 MPa — well below yield (32% of yield)
  Safety factor: 276 / 88 = 3.1 — acceptable

SECTION 5 — GUY WIRE TENSION CALCULATION
================================================================================

FREE-BODY DIAGRAM — GUYED MAST AT LEVEL h_g:
  Wind applies horizontal force F_w above guy level.
  Guy provides horizontal reaction T_h = F_w (for single guy level on windward side).

  Actual tension in guy wire:
    T_guy = F_w / sin(θ)    where θ = angle of guy below horizontal

  Guy angle:
    θ = arctan(h_g / r_anchor)
    At 70% radius anchor: θ = arctan(0.7) = 35°

  For 3-guy configuration: each guy on windward side carries:
    T_guy_each = F_w / (n × sin(θ))   where n = effective number resisting wind
    (typically 1 windward guy at full tension, 2 leeward guys at pre-tension)
    Use n = 1 for conservative calculation.

GUY TENSION TABLE — 70 mph wind, 70% anchor radius (35° guy angle):

  Configuration      | F_wind above  | T_guy (each) | Anchor required
                     | guy level     | n=1 guy      |
  -------------------|---------------|--------------|------------------
  20 ft, 1 guy level | 160 N         | 279 N        | 280 N × 3.0 SF = 840 N
  30 ft, 2nd level   | 290 N (upper) | 505 N        | 505 N × 3.0 SF = 1,515 N
  30 ft, 1st level   | 180 N (lower) | 313 N        | 313 N × 3.0 SF = 940 N
  40 ft, 3rd level   | 450 N (upper) | 783 N        | 783 N × 3.0 SF = 2,350 N
  40 ft, 2nd level   | 310 N (mid)   | 540 N        | 540 N × 3.0 SF = 1,620 N
  40 ft, 1st level   | 200 N (lower) | 348 N        | 348 N × 3.0 SF = 1,045 N

WIRE SAFETY FACTORS:
  3mm Dyneema SK75:     Breaking 1,800 kg (17,650 N) → safety factor on 783 N = 22.5×
  3/16" 7×19 galv. SS: Breaking 1,860 kg (18,240 N) → safety factor = 23.3×
  Both are far in excess of structural requirement; anchor is the limiting factor.

PRE-TENSION REQUIREMENT:
  Each guy wire must be pre-tensioned to prevent sag and lateral oscillation.
  Minimum pre-tension: 50 N (5 kgf) per wire.
  Recommended: 75–100 N (7.5–10 kgf) for masts 20–30 ft.
  This is roughly 10% of wire breaking strength — appropriate for pre-tension.

SECTION 6 — VERTICAL COMPRESSION LOAD ON MAST
================================================================================

COMPRESSION FROM GUY WIRES:
  Each tensioned guy adds a vertical component to mast compression:
    F_vertical = T_guy × cos(θ) = T_guy × cos(35°) = 0.82 × T_guy
  With 3 guys at pre-tension 100 N each:
    F_comp_pretension = 3 × 100 × 0.82 = 246 N per guy level
  At maximum wind tension 783 N (upper guy, 40 ft):
    F_comp_wind = 783 × 0.82 = 642 N (windward guy only compresses mast)

TOTAL MAST COMPRESSION (40 ft, worst case):
  Mast weight:        110 N (11 kg)
  Antenna weight:     50 N (5 kg typical)
  Pre-tension (3 levels × 3 guys × 100 N × 0.82): 740 N
  Wind compression:   3 levels × 642 N: 1,926 N
  Total: 2,826 N (288 kgf)

EULER BUCKLING — 40 ft base section (63mm OD × 3mm wall, 1.5m free length):
  P_cr = π² × E × I / (K × L)²
  E = 68.9 GPa, I = 254,896 mm⁴, K = 0.7 (guyed both ends), L = 1500 mm
  P_cr = π² × 68,900 × 254,896 / (0.7 × 1500)² = 688,406,684 / 1,102,500
       = 624,600 N = 624.6 kN

  Safety factor against buckling: 624,600 / 2,826 = 221×
  Conclusion: Buckling is not a concern for these section dimensions.

SECTION 8 — LIGHTNING RISK AND MITIGATION
================================================================================

ANTENNA HEIGHT RISK:
  An elevated antenna mast is an elevated conductor in an open field.
  Risk increases with height, proximity to storms, and isolation (no nearby trees).
  A 40 ft mast in an open field during a thunderstorm is a SERIOUS hazard.

LIGHTNING MITIGATION:
  1. Lower mast to horizontal or disconnect before any storm approaches.
     Distance rule: thunderstorm risk if you can hear thunder within 10 miles.
     Do not rely on lightning rods — they reduce, not eliminate, strike risk.

  2. If mast cannot be lowered:
     Ground all mast sections via a separate bonding conductor to earth ground.
     Earth ground: copper-clad rod driven ≥2.4m; resistance <25 Ω.
     Bond with #6 AWG minimum solid copper; short direct path (no coils).

  3. Disconnect all feedlines and control cables from radio equipment
     when station is unattended.

  4. Grounding does NOT fully protect antennas or connected equipment.
     Grounding bleeds off static buildup and may offer a preferred path, but
     a direct strike will destroy equipment regardless.

FIRST PRIORITY: LOWER THE MAST. Ground as backup, not substitute.
