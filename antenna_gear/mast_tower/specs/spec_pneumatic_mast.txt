SECTION 1 — CONCEPT AND APPLICATIONS
================================================================================

DESCRIPTION:
  A pneumatic mast uses compressed air or hydraulic fluid pressure to extend
  nested tube sections without manual lift. Deployed with a single air valve
  operation; full height achieved in 15–60 seconds depending on system pressure
  and tube diameter. Extremely useful for rapid deployment in emergency comm,
  tactical field operations, and SOTA/POTA where erecting a guyed mast quickly
  is critical.

CONFIGURATIONS:
  A. Air-over-piston (single tube): One large-bore air cylinder inside a
     structural outer tube. Limited height (~6m / 20 ft) due to cylinder length.

  B. Multi-stage telescoping (nested tubes, air pressure):
     Each nested section is sealed with a piston ring / O-ring; air pressure
     acts on the annular area of each section to extend outward.
     Height range: 6–12m (20–40 ft). Standard commercial "pneumatic mast" design.

  C. Pneumatic winch-assist: Air motor drives cable drum; cable raises a guyed
     push-up mast. More complex; allows arbitrary height with proper cable design.

  D. Hydraulic jack assist: 3-ton bottle jack or hydraulic ram at base; lifts
     base section of guyed mast over a hinged base plate (tilt-up pneumatic).
     Practical for 6–10m (20–33 ft) masts.

COMMERCIAL REFERENCE DESIGNS:
  Aluma Tower / Clark Masts / Comrod pneumatic telescoping masts
  Will-Burt "Night Scan" series (pneumatic LED support — same mechanical principle)
  Crantec series pneumatic masts

SECTION 2 — PNEUMATIC MULTI-STAGE MAST DESIGN
================================================================================

OPERATING PRINCIPLE:
  Nested aluminum tubes, each sealed by a UHMWPE or PTFE piston ring.
  Bottom of each tube receives air pressure from manifold in base.
  Air pressure × annular area = upward force on each section.
  Upper sections extend first (smallest OD, largest annular area ratio).

                    ↑ Section 1 (top, smallest)
                    │  Piston ring seals against inside of Section 2
                    │  F₁ = P × π/4 × (OD₂² − OD₁²)  [annular area]
                    ↓
                    ↑ Section 2 extends second
                    │  Piston ring seals against Section 3
                    │  F₂ = P × π/4 × (OD₃² − OD₂²)
                    ↓
                    │  Section N (base) fixed; air inlet at base

LOCKING:
  Each section locks at full extension via a spring-loaded ball detent or
  annular groove + snap ring. Locked sections support structural loads; air
  pressure is only required for extension, not for standing load.
  (Some designs require continuous pressure — inferior for field use.)

  PREFERRED: Mechanical lock at full extension; air pressure only for raising.
  Field version: section lock pins manually inserted after extension.

AIR SUPPLY REQUIREMENTS:
  Working pressure: 80–120 PSI (5.5–8.3 bar) — standard compressor range
  Volume per extension: 2–5 liters at working pressure (small portable compressor)
  Field sources:
    - Portable 12V compressor (from vehicle 12V or battery pack)
    - Pre-charged SCBA/paintball cylinder (68 cu in / 4500 PSI with regulator)
    - Hand pump (slow but usable for 20 ft systems)
    - CO₂ cartridge: NOT recommended — temperature drop reduces pressure

  SCBA/paintball approach:
    68 cu-in / 4500 PSI cylinder → regulator set to 100 PSI → pilot valve
    One 68 cu-in cylinder provides approximately 40 full-raise operations at 100 PSI

PILOT VALVE:
  3-way valve: RAISE / HOLD / LOWER
    RAISE: Connects air supply to mast base manifold
    HOLD:  Closes supply; mast stays up
    LOWER: Vents mast slowly through needle valve (controlled descent)

  Use 1/4" NPT brass 3-way ball valve. Add inline 0–160 PSI gauge at manifold.

================================================================================
SECTION 3 — PISTON RING / SEAL DESIGN
================================================================================

SEAL MATERIAL:
  UHMWPE (ultra-high molecular weight polyethylene): low friction, self-lubricating
  PTFE (Teflon): lower friction coefficient; harder to machine
  Buna-N O-ring: easy to source; requires lubrication; sufficient for <40°C use
  Viton O-ring: for high-temperature or chemical exposure deployments

PISTON RING DESIGN (printed PETG):
  See mast_collars.scad: pneumatic_piston_ring() module
  Ring OD matches inner diameter of outer tube (snug fit, 0.1–0.2mm clearance)
  Ring groove machined/printed into inner tube upper end
  Air groove: 3mm × 2mm rectangular cross-section O-ring groove
  Lubrication: Light silicone grease applied to sealing surfaces at assembly

SEAL PERFORMANCE:
  Maximum working pressure: 150 PSI with PTFE piston ring
  Leakage rate: <0.5 PSI/minute with properly fitted rings
  Service life: ~500 raise/lower cycles before seal replacement

    3     | 50mm | 44mm  | 3mm  | 1.5m   | 44mm
    4 base| 57mm | 50mm  | 3.5mm| 1.5m   | 50mm

  Nested height: 1.6m (base section 1.5m + end fittings)
  Extended height: ~5.5m (18 ft) after accounting for overlaps and end fittings

EXTENSION FORCE (at 100 PSI = 6.9 bar):
  F₁ = 6.9 × 10⁵ × π/4 × (0.044² − 0.038²) = 6.9e5 × π/4 × 0.000492 = 267 N
  F₂ = 6.9 × 10⁵ × π/4 × (0.050² − 0.044²) = 6.9e5 × π/4 × 0.000564 = 307 N
  F₃ = 6.9 × 10⁵ × π/4 × (0.057² − 0.050²) = 6.9e5 × π/4 × 0.000749 = 408 N

  Net upward force (all stages): 982 N (100 kgf) — sufficient to raise mast + antenna
  Section 1 top-load limit: 267 N ≈ 27 kg — adequate for VHF/UHF antenna + rotator

TIME TO EXTEND:
  At 100 PSI supply from 68 cu-in / 4500 PSI cylinder + regulator:
  Air volume to fill = π/4 × (ID_1² × L₁ + ID_2² × L₂ + ID_3² × L₃) × P_ratio
  Approximate time with 1/4" supply line: 8–15 seconds

SECTION 5 — HYDRAULIC JACK TILT-RAISE VARIANT
================================================================================

CONCEPT:
  A bottle jack or hydraulic ram at the base of a guyed push-up mast raises
  the base section from horizontal to vertical. No special mast construction
  required; standard telescoping sections used. Useful for 20–33 ft masts where
  one operator must erect without a gin pole.

PROCEDURE:
  1. Assemble and extend all mast sections horizontally.
  2. Attach all guys; anchor far-side guy wires.
  3. Position bottle jack under mast at 2m from base pivot.
  4. Jack raises mast; near-side operators manage tag lines.
  5. At 60° angle, mast is self-supporting; tension guys and lower jack.

JACK RATING:
  Moment from mast weight = mass × g × distance to pivot
  30 ft (9m) mast, 8.3 kg, CG at 4.5m from base:
  M = 8.3 × 9.81 × 4.5 = 366 N·m
  At 2m jack arm: F_jack = 366 / 2 = 183 N (19 kgf) — 1.5-ton jack more than sufficient

PNEUMATIC BOTTLE JACK (12V):
  Compact 12V electric or air-over-hydraulic bottle jack
  Allows single-operator raise of mast up to 33 ft (10m)
  Weight: ~3 kg; fits in vehicle toolbox

SECTION 6 — SAFETY REQUIREMENTS FOR PNEUMATIC SYSTEMS
================================================================================

PRESSURE VESSEL SAFETY:
  □ NEVER exceed rated working pressure of air cylinder or tubing
  □ Install pressure relief valve set at 125% working pressure (125 PSI for 100 PSI system)
  □ Inspect all fittings for leaks before pressurizing with soap bubble test
  □ Never use CO₂ cylinders near temperatures <-10°C (pressure drops sharply)
  □ Store pressurized cylinders upright; never in enclosed vehicle in sun

EXTENSION SAFETY:
  □ Clear all personnel from beneath mast during extension
  □ Extension radius = mast height + 20% (stay outside this radius during raise)
  □ Install lower safety strap to prevent uncontrolled collapse if seal fails
  □ Never stand beneath extended mast during windy conditions (>25 mph)

SEAL FAILURE PROTOCOL:
  If rapid air loss occurs while mast is extended:
  1. Immediately move all personnel away from mast (collapse zone)
  2. Do not attempt to manually support or catch mast
  3. Evacuate to distance ≥ mast height from base
  4. Mast will collapse progressively (not explosive); sections fall inward
  5. Inspect sections for damage before reuse
