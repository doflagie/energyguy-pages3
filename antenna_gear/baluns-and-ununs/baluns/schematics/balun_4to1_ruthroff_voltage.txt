================================================================================
BALUN 4:1 VOLTAGE (RUTHROFF) — SIMPLE IMPEDANCE TRANSFORMER
================================================================================

Purpose: Impedance transformation 200Ω balanced → 50Ω unbalanced (4:1)
Type: Voltage balun (ideal transformer approximation)
CM Suppression: Minimal (single core provides some choking action)
Insertion Loss: 0.1–0.15 dB (single core, lower loss than Guanella)
Advantages: Simpler than Guanella, sufficient when low CM current expected

CORE SPECIFICATIONS
================================================================================

HF Configuration (80M–10M):
  Core: Single FT-240-43
  Material: Mix 43 (µ=850, AL=1100 µH/100T)
  Wire: #16 enameled copper
  Primary: 3–4 turns
  Secondary: 6–8 turns (2:1 turns ratio = 4:1 impedance)
  Max Power: 450 W
  Insertion Loss: 0.1–0.15 dB

Band-Specific Turns:
  80M (3.75 MHz):   3 primary, 6 secondary (3:6)
  40M (7.15 MHz):   3 primary, 6 secondary
  30M (10.1 MHz):   3 primary, 6 secondary (slight impedance variance)
  20M (14.2 MHz):   3 primary, 6 secondary
  15M (21.2 MHz):   3 primary, 6 secondary
  10M (28.4 MHz):   3 primary, 6 secondary (upper limit)

Wire Length:
  Total: ~100–130 inches on FT-240-43
  Primary: ~40 inches (3 turns, large core)
  Secondary: ~70 inches (6 turns)

================================================================================
IMPEDANCE TRANSFORMATION THEORY — VOLTAGE BALUN
================================================================================

The Ruthroff voltage balun approximates an IDEAL TRANSFORMER:

Z_out = Z_in / N²   where N = N_secondary / N_primary

Example: 200Ω input, N = 6/3 = 2:1 ratio
  Z_out = 200 / 2² = 200 / 4 = 50Ω ✓

Voltage relationship:
  V_out / V_in = N   (voltage step-down)
  V_out = V_in × (3/6) = V_in / 2

Current relationship:
  I_out / I_in = 1/N   (current step-up)
  I_out = I_in × 2

Power conservation:
  P_in ≈ P_out (ideally, minus resistive loss in wire and ferrite)
  V_in × I_in ≈ V_out × I_out

KEY ASSUMPTION: Ferrite core provides sufficient coupling and inductance
that the transformer approximation holds (99%+ coupling).

================================================================================
WINDING TECHNIQUE — RUTHROFF VOLTAGE BALUN
================================================================================

Materials:
  - 1× FT-240-43 core
  - Spool of #16 enameled copper wire
  - Small vice or mandrel for guiding wire

Step 1: Primary winding (low impedance side)
  - Use slightly thicker gauge (thinner = less space, saves core real estate)
  - Wind 3 turns of primary wire on core
  - Keep turns evenly spaced
  - Ensure wire is not twisted (parallel to axis)
  - Both ends of primary: strip ~0.3" for antenna connections

Step 2: Secondary winding (high impedance side)
  - Without removing primary, wind secondary on TOP of primary
  - Wind 6 turns (2:1 turns ratio)
  - Evenly distribute around remaining core space
  - Both ends of secondary: strip ~0.3" for connector attachment

Step 3: Field connections
  - Primary leg A: Solder to antenna feedpoint leg A
  - Primary leg B: Solder to antenna feedpoint leg B
  - Center tap (between primary and secondary): This connection forms the output reference
  - Secondary center: Solder to coax center conductor
  - Coax braid: Connect to center-tap point (RF ground/balance reference)

Alternative winding order (recommended for better interlacing):
  - Wind primary and secondary INTERLEAVED (every 2nd turn alternate)
  - This improves coupling and reduces leakage inductance

Step 4: Weatherproofing
  - Potting compound optional (voltage balun less temperature sensitive)
  - Enamel insulation usually sufficient for field use

================================================================================
COMMON-MODE SUPPRESSION
================================================================================

Unlike Guanella, Ruthroff has MINIMAL built-in CM suppression:
  Z_CM = 2π × f × L_primary   (only primary inductance counts)

Example @ 80M (3.75 MHz), FT-240-43, 3 turns:
  L_primary = 1100 × 9 / 10000 = 0.99 µH
  Z_CM = 2π × 3.75×10⁶ × 0.99×10⁻⁶ ≈ 23 Ω

This is POOR common-mode suppression (need >250Ω)!

Solution: Add separate 1:1 choke on output coax
  Series ferrite choke (FT-240-43, 12 turns) gives Z_CM ≈ 250 Ω
  Combined system: Voltage transformer + CM choke = balanced approach

================================================================================
ADVANTAGES vs GUANELLA
================================================================================

1. SIMPLICITY:
   - Fewer components (1 core vs 2 stacked cores)
   - Simpler winding pattern
   - Field construction easier

2. LOWER LOSS:
   - Single core ≈ 0.1 dB insertion loss
   - Guanella ≈ 0.20 dB (comparable, but Ruthroff slightly better)

3. COST:
   - Single FT-240-43: ~$12
   - Guanella (2× FT-140-43): ~$15–18
   - Voltage balun cheaper by ~$3–5

4. POWER HANDLING:
   - Less ferrite mass in signal path
   - Slightly better high-power capability (lower core loss)

DISADVANTAGES:
   - Minimal CM suppression (requires secondary choke for balanced use)
   - Narrower bandwidth than Guanella (though still adequate HF)
   - Less common in commercial designs (Guanella preferred for CM suppression)

================================================================================
PRIMARY APPLICATIONS
================================================================================

1. SIMPLE ANTENNA COUPLER INTERFACE:
   - Antenna tuner has low-impedance output (50Ω)
   - Need to match tuner to balanced antenna (200Ω load)
   - Ruthroff provides impedance transformation

2. HIGH-IMPEDANCE LOADS (200Ω–600Ω):
   - When antenna feedpoint is 200Ω balanced
   - Low CM current expected (well-balanced antenna)

3. FIELD EXPEDIENT MATCHING:
   - Portable operation with limited core inventory
   - Single FT-240-43 easier to transport than stacked cores

4. WIRE ANTENNA INTERFACE (with CM choke):
   - Random wire antenna (low CM current if well-grounded)
   - Pair with 1:1 CM choke for complete system

================================================================================
TESTING PROCEDURE — NANOVNA
================================================================================

Setup:
  Port 1: Coax output (50Ω)
  Port 2: Primary input (one leg, reference other leg to ground via 50Ω)
  OR: Use test fixture (50Ω source, 200Ω load, impedance measurement)

Measurement 1 — Insertion Loss (loaded with 200Ω):
  Sweep 1–30 MHz
  Expected: S₂₁ ≈ -0.1 to -0.15 dB
  S₁₁ <-20 dB (excellent match @ design frequency)

Measurement 2 — Impedance Ratio:
  Measure Z_in with 50Ω load on output
  Expected: Z_in ≈ 200Ω ± 10% across band
  Example @ 14.2 MHz: Z_in = 195–205Ω ✓

Measurement 3 — Common-Mode (Primary open):
  Measure S₁₁ from primary to ground (no secondary connection)
  Expected: Z_CM low (10–50Ω) — CONFIRMS that CM choke needed

================================================================================
FAILURE MODES
================================================================================

SYMPTOM: Very high SWR despite proper antenna
ROOT CAUSE 1: Turns ratio wrong (miscounted windings)
SOLUTION: Unwind, recount per winding table, rewind

ROOT CAUSE 2: Primary and secondary windings shorted
SOLUTION: Check ohms from primary terminal to secondary terminal
         Should be ~1–2 Ω (wire resistance) if wound correctly
         <0.1 Ω suggests enamel damaged (unwound/rewind)

SYMPTOM: Excessive heating
ROOT CAUSE 1: High CM current (antenna imbalanced)
SOLUTION: Add 1:1 CM choke (FT-240-43, 12 turns) in series with output

ROOT CAUSE 2: Core loss at high power
SOLUTION: Reduce power, or switch to voltage balun with lower loss

SYMPTOM: Narrower bandwidth than expected
ROOT CAUSE: Design frequency off-target (winding count incorrect)
SOLUTION: Re-count turns, verify against winding table for band

================================================================================
DESIGN EQUATIONS
================================================================================

Impedance Ratio (voltage balun):
  Z_out / Z_in = 1 / N²   where N = N_sec / N_prim

Example calculation:
  Target: 200Ω → 50Ω (4:1 transformation)
  Required ratio: sqrt(200/50) = sqrt(4) = 2:1 turns ratio
  Use: 3 primary, 6 secondary

Insertion Loss (ferrite):
  L_dB ≈ 0.05 + 0.002 × f_MHz   (typical FT-240-43)
  Example @ 14.2 MHz: L ≈ 0.05 + 0.028 = 0.078 dB ✓

Common-Mode Impedance (primary coil alone):
  Z_CM = 2π × f × (AL × N_prim²) / 10000
  For FT-240-43, 3 turns @ 80M: Z_CM ≈ 23 Ω (inadequate)

================================================================================
CONSTRUCTION NOTES
================================================================================

Field Winding Tips:
  - Keep primary turns compact (less capacitance, better coupling)
  - Secondary can be slightly spread (reduces secondary-to-primary capacitance)
  - Interleave every 2–3 turns for best coupling coefficient

Weatherproofing:
  - Enamel insulation on wire adequate for outdoor use
  - Potting optional (epoxy or silicone acceptable)
  - UV protection: Paint or tape if exposed to sun

Assembly:
  - Mount in small plastic or metal box
  - Bring primary to antenna feedpoint via short wires
  - Connect secondary to coax feedline
  - Add ferrite choke on coax (required for CM suppression)

Field Repair:
  - If wire breaks: Solder joint with rosin flux, heat-shrink tubing
  - If enamel damaged: Scrape enamel, re-tin both ends, re-solder
  - If core cracked: Replace core (same material/size)

Cost breakdown:
  - FT-240-43 core: $12
  - Wire (enameled): $2
  - Connectors: $5
  - Potting/enclosure: $3
  - Total: ~$22 (vs commercial $30–50)

================================================================================
RECOMMENDED SYSTEM: VOLTAGE BALUN + 1:1 CHOKE
================================================================================

For most balanced antenna applications:
  - Ruthroff 4:1 voltage balun (impedance transformation)
  - 1:1 ferrite choke (FT-240-43, 12 turns) in series (CM suppression)
  - Total insertion loss: ~0.25 dB
  - Total cost: ~$24
  - Total size: ~3" × 3" × 2" enclosure

This hybrid approach provides:
  ✓ Excellent impedance matching (tight across HF band)
  ✓ Good CM suppression (500+ Ω @ 80M)
  ✓ Low cost
  ✓ Simple construction
  ✓ Adequate power handling (350+ W)
