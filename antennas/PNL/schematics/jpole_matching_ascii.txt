================================================================================
  1. J-POLE ANTENNA THEORY
================================================================================

  The J-pole (Zepp antenna variation) consists of:
    1. A half-wave (λ/2) radiating element
    2. A quarter-wave (λ/4) matching stub (shorted at base)

  The λ/4 stub acts as an impedance transformer between the low-impedance
  feed coax (50Ω) and the high-impedance end-feed point of the λ/2 element.

  1.1  Radiator Impedance

    A half-wave element fed at its end (high-impedance end) presents:

      Z_end ≈ 2500 – 5000 Ω    (high voltage, high impedance)

    This is the top of the matching stub; it connects to the bottom of the
    radiator.

  1.2  Matching Stub Impedance

    The λ/4 shorted stub has input impedance:

      Z_stub_top = ∞  (open circuit at λ/4 from short, parallel resonant)

    At the tap point below the top:

      Z_tap = Z₀_stub × sin(2πd/λ)   [Ω]

    where d = distance from the short (bottom) to the tap point.
    As d increases from 0, Z_tap increases from 0 to Z_stub_top.

    Finding the 50Ω tap:

      d_50 = (λ/4) × (1/π) × arcsin(50/Z₀_stub)   [approximate]

    Practical shortcut: start at d = λ/4 × 0.05 (5% of stub length from short).
    Adjust for SWR minimum.

  1.3  The 1/5 Sizing Rule vs. Foster Reactance

    The J-pole works because the short at the stub base forms a current maximum;
    the tap slides along the voltage standing wave to find 50Ω.

    The stub and radiator share a common conductor (they are parallel wires
    separated by a small gap). The effective length of each is modified by
    mutual coupling. This is why VF correction (0.95) is applied.

  3. POOL NOODLE MOUNTING DETAILS
================================================================================

  The pool noodle provides a rigid, lightweight support for the matching stub wire.
  The stub wire runs in the groove on the outside surface of the noodle.

  Stub shorting bar (bottom):
    Connect both stub wire ends with a 5cm #12 AWG jumper.
    Solder both connections; this is the current maximum point.

  Radiator and stub spacing:
    Maintain 25mm separation between radiator wire and stub wire.
    Use spacers printed from jpole_base.scad every 30–40cm.

================================================================================
  4. MATCHING AND SWR ADJUSTMENT
================================================================================

  4.1  Initial Tap Position

    Connect antenna analyzer to the feed point.
    Start with tap 5% of stub length from short.
    Example: 2m band, stub = 48.8cm → initial tap = 2.4cm from short.

  4.2  Adjustment Procedure

    a. Apply RF from antenna analyzer at band center frequency.
    b. Observe SWR vs. tap position:
       - Moving tap UP (away from short): impedance increases.
       - Moving tap DOWN (toward short): impedance decreases.
    c. Adjust tap for minimum SWR (< 1.5:1 target).
    d. If minimum SWR achieved but > 1.5:1:
       - Verify gap between stub and radiator is not shorted.
       - Verify stub length = λ/4 (±5mm for 2m band).
       - Try slight length adjustment (±5mm) of stub.

  4.3  Common-Mode Choke

    A ferrite bead or coax coil choke is strongly recommended at the coax
    feed point to prevent RF on the coax outer shield:

      Ferrite bead: 1–3 turns of coax on FT-50-43 toroid.
      This is mandatory for clean pattern and SWR stability.

================================================================================
  5. RADIATION PATTERN
================================================================================

  The J-pole has essentially the same pattern as a λ/2 dipole oriented
  vertically: omnidirectional in the horizontal plane with nulls at the poles.

  Gain:
    Free space: ≈ 2.15 dBi (same as dipole)
    Over ground: depends on height; at λ/2 above ground ≈ 5–6 dBi

  For 6m SSB/DX use: mount at maximum achievable height.
  For 2m/1.25m portable: 5m height gives approximately 7–8 dBi effective.
