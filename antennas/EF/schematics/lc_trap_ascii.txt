  Parallel Resonant Traps for Multiband EFQW Roll-Up Antenna

PARALLEL RESONATOR THEORY:
───────────────────────────
A parallel LC circuit resonates at: f₀ = 1 / (2π√LC)

At resonance:
  - Impedance → ∞ (ideal); Ztrap = Q²×R_coil (practical, very high)
  - Acts as an RF "open circuit" in the antenna wire
  - Wire below the trap: radiates as QW on resonant frequency
  - Wire above the trap: electrically disconnected at resonance

Below resonance: trap appears INDUCTIVE (adds electrical length)
Above resonance: trap appears CAPACITIVE (shortens electrical length)

CHOKING FACTOR: Z_trap / Z_wire ≥ 100:1 for adequate isolation
  Example: Z_trap at resonance = Q × √(L/C) = 200 × √(1e-6/127e-12)
         = 200 × 88.7 = 17,740 Ω >> Z_wire (50Ω) → isolation = 23 dB

  SCHEMATIC — TRAP IMPEDANCE vs FREQUENCY (conceptual)
================================================================================

  Z (Ω)
  ▲
  │                      ║
  │                      ║  ← resonant peak (Z = Q²R)
  │                      ║  ≈ 5,000 – 20,000 Ω typical
  │                   ╔══╝
  │                ╔══╝
  │             ╔══╝
  │          ╔══╝
  │       ╔══╝           f₀
  ├───────────────────────────────────────── f
            f₀ ─↑

  Below f₀: Z is inductive (L dominates)
  At f₀:    Z peaks (parallel resonance = open circuit for ideal L,C)
  Above f₀: Z drops capacitively

  PRACTICAL TRAP Q:
  Ferrite cores (Mix 2, Mix 6): Q = 150–250 at HF
  Air core (VHF):               Q = 200–400
  Higher Q = higher Ztrap = better isolation

================================================================================
  TRAP RESONANCE VERIFICATION PROCEDURE
================================================================================

  Using grid dip oscillator (GDO) or antenna analyzer:
  1. Short the output wire of the trap (connect trap output to GND).
  2. Couple GDO to trap toroid (1–2 turns of wire near toroid).
  3. Sweep GDO frequency; note dip in plate current = resonant frequency.
  4. Adjust: Add turns to L1 → lowers f₀; remove turns → raises f₀.
             Increase C1 → lowers f₀; decrease C1 → raises f₀.
  5. Target: f₀ within ±50 kHz of design frequency.
  6. Verify with antenna analyzer: insert trap in series; measure |Z|;
     peak impedance should occur at design frequency.
