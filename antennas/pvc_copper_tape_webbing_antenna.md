**Hybrid PVC + lawn chair webbing antennas** combine the best of both worlds: **Schedule 40 PVC pipe** for rigid structure, mast, and support, plus **3" lawn chair webbing** (flexible, durable, non-conductive strapping) as a substrate for flat/wide conductive tape elements. This creates strong, packable, weather-resistant, and easy-to-build antennas—ideal for portable, stealth, or temporary setups.

You apply **2" copper or aluminum foil tape** to the webbing for the radiating elements (as in your first query), then attach the webbing straps to or around the PVC for support, tensioning, or integration. PVC provides a mast or boom; webbing adds lightweight, rollable elements that won't sag or break easily.

### Practical Hybrid Designs
These build on proven tape/webbing and tape/PVC techniques (e.g., W6NBC-style helical or verticals).

- **Webbing Elements on PVC Mast (Portable Dipole or OCF Vertical)**:  
  Apply copper tape to long strips of 3" webbing to form dipole arms or an off-center-fed (OCF) element. Attach the webbing ends to a PVC cross-arm or T-fitting at the top of a vertical PVC mast (e.g., 1–2" Schedule 40).  
  - Benefits: Wide "fat" elements for better bandwidth; webbing rolls up for storage; PVC acts as mast and feedpoint support. Great for HF (20m–80m) or VHF. Tension the webbing like guy wires or a clothesline for stability.  
  - Variant: No-radial OCF vertical with tape on webbing, hung from PVC.

- **Hybrid Helical / Spirally Loaded Antenna**:  
  Wrap tape-covered webbing in a loose helix around a PVC pipe (or apply straight tape to PVC and reinforce with webbing straps). Or spiral the webbing itself (with tape applied) around the pipe.  
  - This shortens the antenna while maintaining performance (similar to W6NBC's 2m copper tape helix on PVC, but more durable with webbing backing). Excellent for 2m/70cm portables—compact yet efficient.

- **Yagi or Beam with PVC Boom and Webbing Elements**:  
  Use PVC pipe as the boom. Attach parallel strips of tape-on-webbing for driven element, reflectors, and directors (secured with zip ties, clamps, or slots in the PVC).  
  - Webbing allows wider, flatter elements for better gain/bandwidth. Directional and lightweight for VHF/UHF (scanner, ham, or FM). Fold the webbing elements for transport.

- **J-Pole / Slim Jim or Collinear on PVC with Webbing Reinforcement**:  
  Lay the J-shape with tape on a flat webbing section, then wrap or strap it vertically along a PVC mast. Use additional webbing for radials or matching sections.  
  - PVC provides rigidity and weatherproofing (sleeve the base); webbing adds flexibility and strength against wind.

- **Loop or Slot Hybrid**:  
  Form a loop with tape on webbing, then stretch/mount it on a PVC frame (square or rectangular). For slots: Cover webbing with tape, cut a slot, and mount on PVC for elevation.

- **Vertical Monopole with Webbing Radials**:  
  Run a vertical tape strip on webbing up the PVC mast (radiator), then fan out multiple webbing strips with tape at the base for a ground plane. PVC keeps it upright; webbing radials lay flat or angle down.

### Construction Tips
- **Assembly**: Stick 2" tape smoothly onto clean 3" webbing. Secure webbing to PVC with zip ties, hose clamps, UV-resistant tape, or drilled holes. Overlap tape for connections; solder coax where possible (easier with copper).
- **Tuning**: Trim tape lengths; use an SWR meter. PVC slightly affects velocity factor—test assembled.
- **Durability**: Seal edges with more tape or varnish. Webbing resists UV and tearing; PVC adds rigidity. Total weight stays low.
- **Advantages**: More versatile than either material alone—rigid support + flexible/rollable elements. Cheap, stealthy (paint to blend), and packable. Works for VHF/UHF best, scalable to HF.
- **Power/Use**: Low-to-medium power; great for portable (POTA/SOTA), events, or attic/base.

These hybrids aren't widely documented as a specific "type," but they logically extend popular DIY methods with foil tape on flexible/rigid substrates. They're strong performers for the cost.
