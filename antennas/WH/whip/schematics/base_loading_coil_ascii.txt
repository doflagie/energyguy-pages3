1.0  THEORY — WHY THE 5.6m WHIP IS ELECTRICALLY SHORT
================================================================================

A quarter-wave monopole over a perfect ground plane has a feed impedance of
approximately 36.5 Ω resistive at resonance.  The 5.6 m whip achieves this
only near 13 MHz (20m band).  On all lower bands the antenna is electrically
short — its physical length is much less than λ/4.

For a short monopole of length h over ground, the feed impedance is:

    Zin = Rrad + jXin

where:

    Rrad ≈ 40 * (π * h / λ)²      [radiation resistance, ohms]

    Xin  ≈ -jXc  (large capacitive reactance, hundreds to thousands of ohms)

The capacitive reactance of a short monopole can be estimated as:

    Xc ≈ (60 / (h/λ)) * [ln(2h/d) - 1]   [ohms, d = conductor diameter]

For a practical 5.6 m whip (d ≈ 15 mm collapsed, tapered):

    Band    λ/4 (m)   h/λ       Rrad (Ω)   |Xc| (Ω)   Xc/Rrad
    ----    -------   -----     ---------  ---------   -------
    160m    38.29     0.0366    0.124      ~7,400      59,700
    80m     19.14     0.0731    0.484      ~3,500       7,230
    60m     13.44     0.1042    0.977      ~2,310       2,365
    40m      9.57     0.1464    1.758      ~1,540         876
    30m      7.14     0.1961    3.527      ~1,020         289
    20m      5.06     0.2767    ~36.0      ~   50           1.4

The dominance of Xc over Rrad on the low bands means:
  a) The antenna will not accept power without a reactive match.
  b) The system Q is very high, leading to narrow bandwidth.
  c) Ground resistance and coil loss dominate system efficiency.

================================================================================
2.0  LOADING COIL — RESONATING OUT CAPACITIVE REACTANCE
================================================================================

A series loading coil at the base of the whip cancels the antenna's capacitive
reactance:

    XL_required = |Xant|    →    L_required = |Xant| / (2π * f)

The loading coil must resonate with the antenna's self-capacitance.  The coil
itself has series resistance Rcoil = XL / Q_coil.

For a well-wound air-core or powdered-iron coil, Q_coil ≈ 100–200 at HF.
Using Q_coil = 150:

    Rcoil = XL / Q_coil

System efficiency at the loading point (ignoring ground losses):

    Eff = Rrad / (Rrad + Rcoil)   [fraction; multiply by 100 for %]

Ground resistance Rgnd adds further loss.  Assume Rgnd = 5 Ω for a modest
counterpoise (4 × λ/4 radials):

    Eff_total = Rrad / (Rrad + Rcoil + Rgnd)

================================================================================
3.0  L-NETWORK IMPEDANCE MATCHING
================================================================================

After inserting the loading coil the antenna looks like Rfeed = Rrad + Rcoil
(a small resistance, well under 50 Ω).  A low-pass L-network steps up from
Rfeed to 50 Ω:

    Q_L = sqrt( Zout / Zin - 1 )   where Zout = 50 Ω, Zin = Rfeed

    Xs = Q_L * Zin                  (series arm reactance)
    Xp = Zout / Q_L                 (shunt arm reactance)

For a low-pass L (series L toward antenna, shunt C to ground):

    Ls = Xs / (2π * f)
    Cp = 1 / (2π * f * Xp)

NOTE: On some bands the loading coil inductance EXCEEDS Ls, meaning it
provides both the resonating function AND partial L-network matching.
The excess reactance is tuned out by adjusting the shunt capacitor.
A variable capacitor (20–500 pF) at the shunt position covers most cases.

6.0  TAPPED COIL / AUTOTRANSFORMER ALTERNATIVE
================================================================================

Instead of a separate L-network, the loading coil itself can be tapped to
provide impedance matching.  The coil acts as an autotransformer:

    N_tap / N_total = sqrt( Z_coax / Z_ant_at_top )

    Z_ant_at_top ≈ Rrad + Rcoil  (total series resistance at resonance)

Example for 40m (Rrad + Rcoil ≈ 6.9 Ω):

    N_tap / N_total = sqrt( 50 / 6.9 ) = sqrt(7.25) = 2.69

    If N_total = 30 turns,  N_tap = 30 × 2.69 / (N_total ratio) ...

    More directly:  N_tap = N_total × sqrt(Zcoax / Zant)
                          = N_total × sqrt(50 / 6.9)
                          = N_total × 2.69

    Fractional tap: N_tap/N_total = 1/2.69 = 0.372
    Tap at 37% of total turns from ground end.

ADVANTAGE: Single coil, no separate capacitor required.
DISADVANTAGE: Tap is fixed per band; less flexible than variable Cp.
FIELD USE: Mark tap points on coil form with paint marks for each band.

Schematic:

        Coax center ─────────────────────────── Whip
                                  │
                              ╔═══╧════════╗
                              ║ ○○○○○○○○  ║  ← Top (whip end)
                              ║ ○        ○ ║
                              ║ ○  TAP   ○ ║  ← Tap = alligator clip
                              ║ ○  ─────►──║──── Coax center
                              ║ ○        ○ ║
                              ║ ○○○○○○○○  ║  ← Bottom (ground end)
                              ╚═══╤════════╝
                                  │
                               Counterpoise/GND

  The tap is made with a short jumper or an alligator clip to the nearest
  convenient turn.  Trim by adjusting ±1 turn and observing SWR.

8.0  CHOKE BALUN ON FEED LINE
================================================================================

An RF choke (common-mode choke) is required at the feed point to prevent
feedline radiation and maintain pattern symmetry with the counterpoise.

CONSTRUCTION:

  8 turns of RG-174 (or RG-58) coax wound on FT-240-31 toroid:

        │  │  │///│ │///│ │///│ │///│ │///│  │  Coax turns     │

  [FROM XCVR]──Coax──[FT-240-31, 8T RG-174]──[Base loading unit]──[WHIP]
