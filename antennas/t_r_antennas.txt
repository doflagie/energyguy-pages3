  forward/reflected power during transmission. Real-time SWR monitoring.     
  Redundant with nanoVNA but independent verification.

  - Coupling Loop / Magnetic Probe ($20–50, or DIY ~$2) — Non-contact RF     
  field measurement for radiation pattern mapping without direct connection  
  - Dummy Load (1–5W set) ($60) — Safe indoor testing of transmitter +       
  antenna without radiating                                                  
  - RF Attenuators (set) ($50) — Protects instruments from high-power testing
   (reduces 5W to <1W)                                                       
                                                                             
  TIER 3 — Optional Specialized (~$150+):                   
  - Thermal/Infrared Camera ($80–150) — Hot spot detection on antenna,       
  identifies loose connections/high loss                                     
  - Frequency Counter ($30) — Backup frequency measurement (FNIRSI already   
  has this)                                                                  
  - Field Strength Meter ($300–500) — Professional radiation pattern mapping
  (tinySA can do this, but less precise)

● Quadrifilar Helix Antenna (QFH) — Technical Detail                                   
                                                            
  ---                                                                                  
  What It Actually Is
                                                                                       
  Despite the name, a QFH is two bifilar helical loops wound around a cylinder, not
  four independent helices.                                                            
   
  - Loop 1 (large): two wires wound 180° apart, making a half-turn each, connected at  
  top and bottom — forms a closed loop                      
  - Loop 2 (small): same structure, but shorter overall length                         
  - The two loops are angularly offset 90° from each other  
  - Fed 90° out of phase → produces circular polarization                              
                                                                                       
  The large loop is intentionally slightly longer than resonance (inductive, leading   
  current phase). The small loop is slightly shorter (capacitive, lagging). When fed in
   parallel, this reactance difference provides the 90° phasing naturally — no external
   phaser needed for receive.                                                        
                                                                                     
  ---
  Geometry
          
  Each wire element makes a half-turn (180°) around the cylinder over its full axial
  height. Path length per wire:                                                        
   
  wire_length = √( (π × D)² + L² )                                                     
                                                                                       
  Where D = cylinder OD, L = axial height of that loop.                                
                                                                                       
  Design targets:                                                                      
  Large loop total (2 wires): 2 × wire_length_L ≈ 1.085λ                             
  Small loop total (2 wires): 2 × wire_length_S ≈ 0.915λ                               
                                                                                       
  Solving for L given chosen D:                                                      
  L_L = √( (0.5425λ)² - (πD)² )                                                        
  L_S = √( (0.4575λ)² - (πD)² )

  ---                                                                                  
  Marking the Helix on PVC                                  
                                                                                       
  A half-turn helix unrolls to a straight diagonal line. To mark it:
                                                                                       
  1. Cut a piece of paper exactly πD wide × L tall                                     
  2. Wrap paper around pipe — edges butt exactly once around                           
  3. Draw a straight diagonal line from bottom-left to top-right (for RHCP winding)    
  4. Tape paper; punch through at both ends with a pin to mark positions on pipe       
  5. Remove paper, connect the two pin-marks with a felt-tip line directly on the PVC  
                                                                                       
  For RHCP (standard for satellite downlinks): wire goes counterclockwise when traced  
  from bottom to top (right-hand rule, thumb pointing away from Earth).                
                                                                                       
  Each loop needs two such lines, 180° apart on the cylinder. Rotate the paper 180° and
   repeat.                                                                           
                                                                                       
  ---                                                       
  Copper Tape Application                                                            
                         
  - Use 1/2" or 3/4" tape on pipe surfaces — 2" tape will buckle on curves
  - Apply tape along the marked helix line on the PVC OD                               
  - At top and bottom, bridge the two wires of each loop with a short tape tab — this  
  forms the closed loop                                                                
  - The bridging tabs at top are just open (high impedance); the bridge at bottom is   
  where the feedpoint is for the small loop, or the short circuit for the large loop   
  depending on your feed approach                                                    
                                                                                       
      TOP:                                                                             
      ──────────────────────────────                                                 
      Wire 1A ───(copper tab)─── Wire 1B    ← large loop top bridge (shorted)          
      Wire 2A ───(copper tab)─── Wire 2B    ← small loop top bridge (shorted)          
                                                                                     
      BOTTOM (feedpoint):                                                              
      Wire 1A ──── FEED LARGE ──── Wire 1B                  
      Wire 2A ──── FEED SMALL ──── Wire 2B                                             
                                                                                       
  ---                                                                                
  Feed Network                                                                         
                                                            
  Option 1 — Self-phasing parallel feed (simplest, receive only)                     
                                                                                       
  Connect both loops in parallel at the bottom feedpoint. The inductive/capacitive     
  reactance difference produces approximate 90° phasing. Sufficient for weather        
  satellite APT reception.                                                             
                                                                                     
      coax center ───┬─── large loop bottom                                          
                     └─── small loop bottom                                            
      coax shield ───┴─── common ground (both loops)
                                                                                       
  Add a common-mode choke (5 turns of coax on FT-240-43) at the feedpoint.             
                                                                                       
  Option 2 — Coax phaser (better circular polarization purity)                         
                                                                                     
  Feed both loops from separate coax runs, one with an extra λ/4 section inserted:     
                                                            
      T-connector at radio end                                                         
      ├── coax A (length L) ──────────────── large loop     
      └── coax B (length L + λ/4 × VF) ──── small loop                                 
   
  At 137.5 MHz, λ/4 in RG-58 (VF=0.66) = 2182/4 × 0.66 = 360mm of extra coax.          
                                                            
  Option 3 — LNA at feedpoint (best for weather sat RX)                                
                                                            
  Mount a filtered 137 MHz LNA (RTL-SDR Blog V4 LNA, LNA4ALL, or similar) directly at  
  the feedpoint before any coax run. The LNA overcomes coax loss. Self-phasing feed is
  adequate when the LNA noise figure dominates.                                        
                                                            
  ---                                                                                
  Pattern and Polarization
                          
  - Gain: ~2–3 dBi (hemispherical, not a gain antenna)
  - Pattern: peaks at 20–40° elevation, wraps horizon to past zenith                   
  - Null at zenith (minor, barely noticeable in practice during satellite pass)
  - Axial ratio: 1–3 dB (good circular polarization, not perfect)                      
  - No tracking required — covers the entire sky above the horizon                     
                                                                                       
  This is the correct pattern shape for a LEO satellite pass: the satellite rises from 
  the horizon, climbs to max elevation (rarely directly overhead), and sets on the     
  other side. The QFH covers the entire pass with minimal gain variation.              
                                                                                     
  ---                                                                                
  Construction Notes Specific to Copper Tape on PVC
                                                                                       
  Soldering copper tape to PVC: The tape is solderable. Apply flux, heat quickly — PVC
  distorts around 70–80°C so don't linger. Keep iron contact under 2 seconds per joint.
   Pre-tin coax leads before attaching to tape.             
                                                                                       
  Tape width vs. pipe circumference: The tape width should be less than 10% of the pipe
   circumference to avoid overlap issues when following the helix curve. For 4" pipe 
  (circumference = 359mm): use ≤ 36mm (1.5") tape. 3/4" tape is ideal.                 
                                                            
  Structural support: The copper tape has enough adhesion to stay on PVC permanently in
   the field. Run a strip of packing tape over the helix tape as weather protection; it
   won't significantly affect RF at 137 MHz.                                           
                                                            
  Feed pigtail: Solder a short length of RG-58 directly to the bottom loop ends. Keep  
  the pigtail under 50mm. Route coax straight down the outside of the pipe; secure with
   zip ties. Add choke at the feedpoint.
