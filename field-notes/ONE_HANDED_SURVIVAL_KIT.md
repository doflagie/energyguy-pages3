# Non-Threatening One-Handed Survival Kit Design

---

## DESIGN PRINCIPLES

**One-handed operation:** Tools designed for grip-and-twist, lever action, or gravity-assist. No items requiring two-handed setup or tension holds.

**Non-threatening appearance:** Camping/outdoors aesthetic; nothing tactical, aggressive, or weapons-like. Blends into hiking/fishing gear.

**Modularity:** Components nest together or attach to belt/backpack for quick access with one hand.

---

## 1. NAVIGATION & ORIENTATION

### Magnetized Needle Compass
- **Item:** Surgical steel needle (0.8mm dia, 38mm long)
- **Magnetization:** Stropped 30× on neodymium magnet (N52, retain magnetism indefinitely)
- **Storage:** Cork float + sealed vial (floats in cup of water = makeshift compass)
- **One-handed use:** Float in water, read from above. No moving parts, no calibration.
- **Non-threatening:** Looks like sewing needle

### Backup: Magnetized Metal Ring
- **Item:** Small stainless ring (25mm dia) magnetized the same way
- **Use:** Floats on leaf in water, spins to north
- **Advantage:** Works if needle lost
- **Storage:** Key ring attachment

### Polaris Finder (Night Navigation)
- **Item:** Laminated 2"×3" card showing Polaris location via Big Dipper pointer
- **One-handed:** Hold up to eye, identify stars, read Polaris position
- **Non-threatening:** Looks like guide card

## 2. FIRE MAKING (Redundant Methods)

### Primary: Ferro Rod + Striker
- **Item:** 5mm × 50mm ferro rod (1000+ strikes)
- **Striker:** Spring steel loop attached to rod with paracord lanyard
- **One-handed use:** Hold rod vertical in teeth or with knees, strike downward with striker
- **Alternative grip:** Rod in one hand, strike against boot heel or rock edge
- **Non-threatening:** Looks like camping tool (because it is)

### Secondary: Flint & Steel
- **Item:** 2"×1" flint stone + carbon steel scraper (L-shaped)
- **One-handed:** Hold flint in one hand, strike with steel in other, sparks land on tinder
- **Backup:** Works wet, ancient proven method
- **Non-threatening:** Just rocks and metal

### Tertiary: Waterproof Matches
- **Item:** 20× strike-anywhere matches in sealed tube
- **Storage:** Wax-sealed aluminum tube (waterproof, lightweight)
- **One-handed:** Strike on included striker pad, light tinder
- **Non-threatening:** Standard camping item

### Quaternary: Lighter
- **Item:** Single disposable butane lighter (backup only)
- **Storage:** Sealed in waterproof bag
- **One-handed:** Thumb ignition
- **Non-threatening:** Universal camping tool

## 3. FIRE STARTER MATERIALS (Tinder Kit)

### Char Cloth
- **Item:** 2"×2" squares of cotton fabric pre-charred
- **Prep:** Soak in saltpeter solution, dry, char in anaerobic container
- **Use:** Catches spark from ferro rod immediately
- **One-handed:** Pull from pouch, place on rock, strike into it
- **Storage:** Small ziplock in main kit

### Cotton Balls + Petroleum Jelly
- **Item:** 10× cotton balls coated with vaseline
- **Use:** Long burn time (5+ min), water-resistant
- **One-handed:** Pinch apart, light edge
- **Storage:** Zip bag

### Dry Grass Bundle
- **Item:** Pre-gathered in field, dried, bundled
- **Use:** Catches spark, builds to kindling
- **One-handed:** Lay down, strike sparks into it, gradually add sticks
- **Non-threatening:** Just dried grass

### Fatwood Splinters
- **Item:** 5–10 small resinous pine splinters
- **Use:** Self-lighting, high oil content, catches spark easily
- **One-handed:** Lay out, spark into bundle
- **Storage:** Small bag

## 4. KNIFE (Non-Threatening Design)

### Primary: Kitchen-Style Fixed Blade
- **Item:** 3.5" straight kitchen/utility blade, high-carbon steel
- **Design:** Rounded tip (not aggressive), ergonomic handle
- **Sheath:** Leather horizontal carry (on belt, accessible one-handed)
- **One-handed use:**
  - Draw with thumb from sheath
  - Pinch grip or hammer grip for cutting/chopping
  - Reverse grip (thumb on spine) for detail work
  - Secure on block or between knees to cut with one hand
- **Non-threatening:** Looks like camping/cooking knife (because it is), not tactical

### Handle Design (Critical for One-Handed)
- **Diameter:** 1.25" (full hand grip comfortable)
- **Material:** Hardwood (aesthetically non-threatening)
- **Cross-guard:** Small lip (prevents hand from sliding forward)
- **Pommel:** Rounded ball (tool for impact, doesn't slide out)

### Maintenance One-Handed
- **Sharpening stone:** Belt-loop mounted, secure
- **Technique:** Draw blade across stone toward body, trapped in horizontal mount
- **No separate honing steel needed** (single-stage maintenance)

## 5. COOKING STOVE

### Primary: Alcohol Stove (One-Handed Operation)
- **Item:** 16oz aluminum can converted stove
- **Design:** Holes punched around top edge (no moving parts)
- **Fuel:** Denatured alcohol in sealed bottle
- **One-handed use:**
  - Set stove on flat rock
  - Pour fuel from bottle into stove with one hand
  - Light with match/ferro rod
  - Place pot on top
  - Regulate by covering/uncovering vent holes with finger
- **Non-threatening:** Looks like camping stove
- **Advantage:** No fragile burners, no pressurized fuel, completely safe

### Secondary: Hexamine Tablets (Esbit)
- **Item:** 6× tablet fuel blocks (20g each, ~1400 calories heat per block)
- **Use:** Emergency backup, no liquid to carry
- **One-handed:** Stack tablets, light, place pot on folding grill
- **Non-threatening:** Looks like camping fuel tablets (because they are)
- **Drawback:** Heavier than alcohol for equivalent heat

### Folding Grill (Necessary for Both)
- **Item:** Lightweight titanium or aluminum cross-brace (folds flat)
- **Design:** Two interlocking L-brackets, unfolds to 4-leg support
- **One-handed:** Snap together vertically, one hand, takes 5 seconds
- **Holds:** Standard pot or cup

## 6. SHELTER SYSTEM

### Primary: Lightweight Tarp
- **Item:** 5'×7' ultralight ripstop nylon, silnylon coating (3–4 oz)
- **Color:** Tan, olive, or forest (non-threatening, blends in)
- **Design:** Simple rectangle, reinforced corner grommets
- **One-handed deployment:**
  - Lay flat on ground
  - Tie one corner to nearby tree/rock (paracord already attached)
  - Lift opposite corner with one hand, secure
  - Slide under if rain imminent (no complex rigging)
  - Works as lean-to, A-frame, or ground cover

### Secondary: Ground Sheet
- **Item:** 4'×6' heavy-mil plastic sheet or groundcloth
- **Use:** Moisture barrier under sleeping area
- **One-handed:** Unfold, position under tarp
- **Non-threatening:** Looks like camping mat

### Rigging: Paracord System
- **Item:** 25 feet paracord (550 type, carries 550 lb test)
- **Pre-configured:** Three 8-foot sections with loop knots on each end
- **One-handed tie:**
  - Clove hitch: wrap rope around post, loop through itself, pull tight (one hand after initial wrap)
  - Taut-line hitch: adjustable loop for tarp corners, works with one hand
  - Bowline: one-handed knot (pull loop through, twist, feed rope)

## 7. SLEEPING SYSTEM

### Sleeping Bag (Ultra-Lightweight)
- **Item:** Synthetic 40°F quilt (~1.5 lbs, 700 fill power)
- **Design:** Rectangular, no hood (avoids claustrophobia, quicker to use one-handed)
- **One-handed use:**
  - Unroll on ground
  - Slide in one-handed
  - Drawstring closes with one hand
  - No zippers (one-handed zipping is difficult)
- **Advantage:** Lighter than traditional bags, versatile temperature range

### Sleeping Pad (Insulation + Comfort)
- **Item:** Lightweight foam pad (R-value 2, ~0.5 lbs) OR ultralight inflatable (1 lb with pump)
- **If inflatable:** Use integrated hand pump (one-handed air release)
- **If foam:** Pre-roll for quick deployment
- **One-handed:** Unroll, lie on top of ground sheet, under quilt

## 14. ONE-HANDED TECHNIQUES (Key Skills)

### Fire Starting (One-Handed Master)
1. Gather tinder with one hand into a pile
2. Strike ferro rod *downward* while rod is held in:
   - Mouth/teeth (for stability)
   - Wedged between knees
   - Clamped in boot between sole and heel
3. Sparks fall onto tinder below
4. Build fire from tinder → kindling → fuel wood

### Shelter Setup (One-Handed)
1. Lay tarp on ground
2. Tie one corner to tree/rock (loop pre-tied, just pull tight)
3. Lift opposite corner, tie same way
4. Crawl under or prop up one edge with stick

### Cooking (One-Handed)
1. Set stove on stable rock
2. Pour fuel bottle with one hand (other hand free)
3. Light with match
4. Place pot on grill with one hand
5. Stir with spork while gripping pot handle

### Cutting (One-Handed)
- **Secure method:** Clamp item between knees or under boot toe
- **Cut toward yourself:** Knife hand works, body stabilizes item
- **Avoid:** Holding both knife and food; trap one between knees

### Knot-Tying (One-Handed Knots)
- **Bowline:** Can tie with one hand (though two-hand faster)
- **Clove hitch:** Wrap rope, loop through, pull (one-handed after initial wrap)
- **Taut-line hitch:** Adjustable loop, one-handed after setup
- **Practice:** Pre-tie critical knots before field use

## 15. NON-THREATENING SAFETY

### Appearance
- No black tactical gear (use tan/olive/khaki)
- No knife sheaths with aggressive branding
- No military-style carriers
- No spike/serrated/combat designs
- Standard camping knife, not bowie or tactical fixed blade

### Legal Considerations
- **Check local laws:** Blade length limits (usually 3–4" limit for fixed knives)
- **Cordage:** No restrictions (always legal)
- **Fire-making:** Ferro rod/flint = legal everywhere
- **Matches:** Legal
- **Lighter:** Legal

### Presentation
- Kit looks like camping/outdoor gear (because it is)
- Carry bag labeled "Field Kit" or "Survival Kit" (educational, transparent)
- Appear competent and organized (not aggressive or paranoid)

## 17. CW COMMUNICATIONS (One-Handed)

### KY-116/U Leg Clamp + J-37 Key

The KY-116/U is a military leg clamp platform that straps to the thigh,
holding a telegraph key firmly against the leg. Designed specifically for
field operation where no table is available — standing, seated on the
ground, or in a vehicle. The J-37 straight key is the standard companion
for this clamp; the combination is purpose-matched.

**Ergonomics:**
- Forearm rests naturally on the thigh, wrist relaxed
- Downstroke uses arm weight + gravity rather than fighting gravity as
  with a desk key
- Key action uses minimal wrist/finger motion — less joint stress
  than desk position for extended operating sessions
- Therapeutic benefit for straight key practice: natural arm position
  reduces fatigue and repetitive stress compared to table height

**One-handed operation:**
- Key is on the leg — operating hand rests naturally on the key
- Other hand completely free for rig controls, logging, map, antenna
- Stable platform that moves with the operator rather than being
  separate from them

**Three field positions — all operational:**
- **Moving vehicle**: platform vibration irrelevant; key and hand move together
- **Foxhole / seated on ground**: thigh is the table; no surface needed
- **Standing**: key on leg, sending hand on key, other hand completely free

**Vehicle and rough terrain stability (spring clamp variant):**
The metal spring clamp couples the key directly to the operator's body
mass. Road vibration, rough terrain, or platform motion moves the operator
and the key together — there is no relative motion between the hand and
the key contact. A table-mounted key in a moving vehicle bounces
independently of the operator, making clean CW difficult or impossible.
With the leg clamp, the key is rock steady at speed on rough roads.
This makes the spring clamp variant significantly more capable than a
desk key for mobile or field-portable operation.

These were used by RTO operators sending CW from helicopters in Vietnam.
Helicopter vibration is broadband, high-amplitude, and unpredictable —
the leg clamp worked there, which is the definitive proof of the design.
The body-coupling principle scales down to any lesser platform.

**Related items:**
- **J-38**: Dimensionally similar to J-37, more common surplus item,
  fits the KY-116/U identically — easier to find and parts are available
- **J-45**: Factory combination — J-38 mounted directly into an integral
  leg clamp base, issued as a single unit. If found, grab it.
- **J-37 vs J-38**: Either works on the KY-116/U; the J-38 is the more
  available item

**Military history:**
The leg clamp key is Signal Corps equipment designed from the start for
operation without a fixed surface. The concept predates Vietnam — WWII
operators used leg clamp keys in jeeps, foxholes, and ship compartments
where no table was available or practical. The key rides on the operator,
not on the platform.

Vietnam saw the design prove itself under the hardest conditions: CW
operators sending from UH-1 Hueys at low altitude, in combat, with
everything vibrating. A table-mounted key in that environment is useless.
The leg clamp worked because the key and the operator are a single
coupled system — the vibration that would defeat a desk key is the same
vibration the operator's hand is already riding.

The same principle applied in jeeps on rough roads, in the back of trucks,
and in any situation where the operator had no stable external surface but
did have a leg. The design is elegant in its simplicity: strap the problem
to the solution.

Three classic Signal Corps operating positions, all covered by the leg clamp:
- **Moving vehicle** (jeep, truck, helicopter): platform vibration irrelevant
- **Foxhole / seated on ground**: no table, no problem — thigh is the table
- **Standing**: key on the leg, hand on the key, other hand free for weapon,
  map, or radio controls

Any position a soldier can hold, the leg clamp key can operate from.

**Availability:** KY-116/U and J-37/J-38 are consistent eBay and hamfest
items. The keys are nearly indestructible. Two variants exist: strap
versions (canvas or leather) and metal spring clamp versions. The spring
clamp variant grips the thigh directly via spring tension — faster to
attach and remove than strap versions, no buckles to manage one-handed.
Inspect spring tension and clamp pivot on the spring clamp variant; the
key mechanism itself rarely fails.

**Integration with field kit:**
- Spring clamps to thigh over trousers — attaches and releases one-handed,
  no table, no flat surface required
- Coax pigtail from key runs to rig (QRP transceiver, Michigan Mighty
  Mite, Tuna Tin, etc.) carried in pack or on belt
- Combined with a crystal-controlled QRP rig: fully self-contained
  one-handed HF CW station operable from any position

## 18. PEDESTRIAN QRP ANTENNA

Two modes with different answers.

### Mode 1: Stop-and-Operate (SOTA/POTA Style)

Walk to position, deploy, operate, pack up. Near-full efficiency.

**Linked EFHW + telescoping fishing pole**
- **Pole**: 6–7m fiberglass telescoping, collapses to ~60cm, ~150g
  Leans against a tree, stakes into soft ground, ties to a fence post
- **Wire**: Linked EFHW — 20m segment for 40m, link back to 10m for 20m
  Hangs from pole tip, runs out at slight angle
- **Feed**: 49:1 unun at base, short coax to rig, 1m counterpoise clipped
  to unun ground terminal
- **Setup time**: 5 minutes
- **Weight**: ~350g total (pole + wire + unun)
- **Efficiency**: Near full dipole on linked bands

Natural pairing with the leg clamp key: extend the pole, sit on a rock,
clamp key to thigh, operate. The foxhole position from the Signal Corps
checklist.

### Mode 2: Pedestrian Mobile (Walking and Transmitting)

The manpack radio problem. Solved the same way by the military for decades.

**Short loaded whip on pack frame + trailing counterpoise**
- Mount a 1–1.5m loaded whip vertically on pack frame, above head height
- Clip a counterpoise wire to pack waist belt — trail along ground behind
- ATU-100 for matching (radiation resistance will be 2–10Ω at this
  whip length — ATU earns its keep here)
- Coax runs from whip down to rig in chest or hip pack

Efficiency is poor — 15–30% on 40m — but at 5W that is 0.75–1.5W
effective radiated. QRP CW contacts are possible with a patient operator.
This is exactly the AN/PRC-104 and HF manpack approach. It works.

### Comparison

  Mode              Antenna               Efficiency   Setup      Key position
  ----------------  --------------------  -----------  ---------  ---------------
  Stop-and-operate  Linked EFHW + pole    ~90%         5 minutes  Seated (foxhole)
  Pedestrian mobile Loaded whip on pack   15–30%       None       Walking/standing

### Linked Counterpoise

The counterpoise can be linked the same way as the EFHW wire — break
points at λ/4 positions for each band. Link positions match the antenna:

  Band   EFHW link   Counterpoise link   Active counterpoise
  -----  ----------  -----------------   -------------------
  20m    L1 open     L1 open             5.3m
  40m    L1 closed   L1 closed           10.5m
  80m    L1+L2 closed L1+L2 closed       30.2m

Band change = close or open the same-numbered clip on both wires.
One action on the antenna, one action on the counterpoise.

For the 49:1 unun EFHW, the floating secondary means a short counterpoise
(1–2m) works across multiple bands without linking — linking matters more
for the 5.6m whip where feed impedance is more sensitive to ground system
length. See counterpoise.md for full detail.

### Integration with Kit

The two modes complement each other. Carry the fishing pole and linked
EFHW for positions where 5 minutes is available. Mount a short loaded
whip on the pack for on-the-move operation. Same leg clamp key, same rig,
swap antenna as the situation allows.

## 19. WHEELCHAIR QRP ANTENNA

The wheelchair is a different platform from a vehicle or a walking operator
— less ground plane than a car, more stable than walking, feed point
naturally near ground level, and reach is limited for one-handed setup.

### The Platform Advantage

A wheelchair operator is naturally at the base of the antenna. A
ground-level feed point with radials on the ground is standard vertical
antenna design — the geometry is already correct.

### Mode 1: Fixed Position Operation

**Telescoping fishing pole in a RAM mount on the wheelchair frame**

RAM mounts (ball-and-socket articulating mounts) clamp onto wheelchair
tubes — the same hardware used for phone mounts and cup holders. A RAM
mount holds the fishing pole vertically off the wheelchair arm or frame.

- Pole extends 6–7m vertically from chair
- Linked EFHW hangs from tip, slopes away from chair
- 49:1 unun at chair level, coax to rig in lap or side bag
- 2–4 radials staked into ground with tent pegs around the position —
  reachable from chair height without bending far
- Setup is essentially one-handed: extend pole, clip wire, push tent
  pegs, connect coax

### Mode 2: Rolling and Operating

**Loaded whip on RAM mount + counterpoise clipped to frame**

- 1–1.5m loaded whip in a RAM mount on the frame
- Counterpoise wire clipped to frame, trailing or coiled on footrest
- ATU-100 for matching
- Same principle as pedestrian mobile whip — wheelchair is the platform,
  operator and chair move together
- More stable than walking — less vibration, smoother motion

### Ground System from a Wheelchair

The wheelchair frame is metal but small — not the ground plane a car
provides. Compensate with:

- **Radials on the ground**: For 40m, λ/4 = 10m per radial. Stake with
  tent pegs — push in with heel of hand or boot toe, no mallet needed
  in soft ground. 2–4 radials significantly improve efficiency.
- **Counterpoise clipped to frame**: Shorter, less efficient, but
  requires no staking — useful for rolling operation.
- **Concrete/pavement**: Poor ground. Add more radials or longer
  counterpoise.
- **Grass/soil**: Good ground. Even 2 radials make a noticeable
  difference.

### Comparison

  Mode            Antenna                  Efficiency   Setup
  --------------  -----------------------  -----------  ----------
  Fixed position  Linked EFHW + pole       ~85%         5–10 min
  Rolling         Loaded whip 1.5m         20–35%       None
  Fixed, minimal  Random wire + 9:1 unun   ~70%         3 min

### Key Hardware

- **RAM mount**: B-size ball, tube clamp base — fits standard wheelchair
  tubing, locks one-handed
- **Fishing pole**: same 6–7m fiberglass pole as pedestrian kit (section
  18) — dual use, one item serves both modes
- **Tent pegs**: push in with heel of hand or boot toe
- **Pre-clipped radials**: radial wires with alligator clips already
  attached, coiled in pouch — deploy and clip without tools

## TESTING ONE-HANDED COMPETENCE

Before field use, practice:
1. ✓ Start fire with ferro rod, teeth holding rod
2. ✓ Tie tarp with one hand (both corners)
3. ✓ Cook meal, stir pot while holding handle
4. ✓ Cut food secured between knees
5. ✓ Braid cordage one-handed
6. ✓ Dig cathole with trowel, one-handed
7. ✓ Navigate by sun altitude (hand-fist method), then magnetized needle
8. ✓ Identify 5 local edible plants from ID cards
9. ✓ Deploy shelter in <5 minutes, one-handed
10. ✓ Complete session (cook, eat, clean, sleep, pack) entirely one-handed
11. ✓ Clamp KY-116/U to thigh one-handed, send 5-word message, release clamp
12. ✓ Send readable CW while riding over rough ground (key steady = test passed)
13. ✓ Send readable CW in highest-vibration platform available — if it works in a
       helicopter it works anywhere (Vietnam RTO standard)
14. ✓ Send from each classic Signal Corps position: moving vehicle, seated on ground
       (foxhole), standing — confirm key is stable in all three (WWII through Vietnam
       field standard)
15. ✓ Fixed wheelchair position: deploy fishing pole + linked EFHW from RAM mount,
       stake 2 radials one-handed, make contact — complete setup without standing
16. ✓ Rolling wheelchair: operate loaded whip + counterpoise while moving, confirm
       key and antenna stable from chair platform
17. ✓ Measure R_ground at valley floor, foothill, and ridgeline sites — compare to
       M3 map predictions; log σ and season to build empirical local conductivity map
18. ✓ Complete site log entry (date, location, soil condition, R_ground, inferred σ,
       M3 delta) for each of the four seasonal windows — wet, declining, dry, recovering
19. ✓ Print R_ground_site_log_card.pdf on card stock, laminate, verify grease pencil
       writes and wipes cleanly — card ready before first field session

Once competent with these, you're genuinely self-sufficient in field scenarios.
