CHAPTER 1 — GENERAL INFORMATION

1-1. SCOPE

This manual covers design, construction, and calibration of a laboratory-grade audio oscillator producing sine and square wave outputs from 20 Hz to 100 kHz. Primary applications include audio amplifier testing, filter response characterization, and modulation source for AM/FM modulators.

1-2. APPLICABLE DOCUMENTS

TM-TGEAR-012 (Function Generator), TM-TGEAR-020 (Oscilloscope), TM-CAL-006 (Voltage Reference).

1-3. SAFETY SUMMARY

WARNINGIf this oscillator is powered from AC mains, use an isolation transformer to prevent ground loops and shock hazard. The output ground must not be connected to an earthed chassis while the oscillator ground floats at mains potential.

CHAPTER 2 — THEORY OF OPERATION

2-1. WIEN BRIDGE OSCILLATOR

The classical Wien bridge uses an RC frequency-selective positive-feedback network around an op-amp. Oscillation occurs when the lag network phase shift is exactly 0° (net loop phase = 360°) and loop gain = 1.

Wien bridge oscillation frequencyf = 1 / (2π × R × C)

A thermistor or FET in the feedback controls amplitude automatically via AGC (automatic gain control). Without AGC, the oscillator clips into a square wave.

2-2. SQUARE WAVE GENERATION

A Schmitt-trigger comparator following the sine output produces a rail-to-rail square wave. Rise time is determined by the comparator slew rate; for the LM393, tr ≈ 200 ns, limiting usable bandwidth to ∼500 kHz.

2-3. OUTPUT AMPLITUDE CONTROL

An op-amp output stage (unity-gain buffer) drives a 10-turn cermet potentiometer as a continuous level control. Peak output voltage is typically ±5 V into high impedance, ±3 V into 600 Ω load.

CHAPTER 3 — CONSTRUCTION

3-1. TOPOLOGY SELECTION

For maximum spectral purity (THD <0.1%), use a Wien bridge with lamp or JFET AGC. For simplicity, use a relaxation oscillator (555 or XR-2206 IC), which yields THD 0.5–1%.

CHAPTER 4 — ASSEMBLY PROCEDURES

  1. Solder Wien bridge RC network components. Use 1% film capacitors; ceramic capacitors have poor temperature stability.
  2. Install JFET AGC circuit. Verify VGS rests at −0.5 to −1.5 V during oscillation for linear AGC action.
  3. Install output buffer op-amp and level potentiometer.
  4. Power on with current-limited supply. Oscillation should start within 1 second. If it does not start, check that loop gain > 1 by temporarily reducing feedback attenuation.
  5. Connect output to oscilloscope. Verify waveform is sinusoidal, THD <1% (no visible flat tops or crossover distortion).

CHAPTER 5 — CALIBRATION PROCEDURE

5-1. FREQUENCY CALIBRATION

  1. Connect oscillator output to frequency counter (TM-TGEAR-010). Set oscillator to 1 kHz range.
  2. Trim R or C in Wien bridge until display reads 1000 ± 1 Hz. Document actual value.
  3. Repeat at 10 kHz and 100 kHz. Log results vs. dial marking.
  4. Cross-check with GPS-disciplined counter per TM-CAL-002 for highest accuracy.

5-2. AMPLITUDE CALIBRATION

  1. Set output level control to maximum. Connect precision DMM (TM-TGEAR-017, AC V range) across output.
  2. Record VRMS. Typical: 2.0–5.0 VRMS.
  3. Verify level control provides smooth attenuation from max to 0 with no dead zones.

CHAPTER 6 — TUNING AND ADJUSTMENT

Frequency range switching is accomplished by switching R or C values in decade steps (10× per range). Use rotary switch banks with matched film capacitors. AGC set-point (output amplitude) is adjusted by the ratio R4/R5 in the AGC loop; increasing R4 raises output amplitude.
