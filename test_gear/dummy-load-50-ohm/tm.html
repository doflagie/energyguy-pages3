CHAPTER 1 — GENERAL INFORMATION

1-1. SCOPE

This manual covers construction and calibration of a 50 Ω non-reactive dummy load. The dummy load absorbs transmitter output power for testing without radiating RF, protecting connected equipment and complying with FCC Part 97 requirement that transmitters be tested into a suitable non-radiating load.

1-2. APPLICABLE DOCUMENTS

TM-CAL-014 (Dummy Load Standard), TM-TGEAR-023 (RF Power Meter), TM-TGEAR-027 (SWR Meter), TM-CAL-015 (Attenuator Pads).

CAUTIONDo not exceed the power rating of the dummy load. Resistors will fail open or short circuit if overloaded. At 100 W, a non-oil-cooled load requires forced air cooling or immersion in mineral oil.

CHAPTER 2 — THEORY OF OPERATION

2-1. RESISTIVE LOAD

An ideal 50 Ω dummy load presents a purely resistive impedance at all frequencies, resulting in zero reflected power and SWR = 1.0:1. Real loads deviate at VHF/UHF due to resistor lead inductance and shunt capacitance.

SWR from load impedanceSWR = |Zload + Z0| / |Zload − Z0| [simplified for purely real Zload]

2-2. PARALLEL RESISTOR NETWORKS

Using multiple resistors in parallel improves high-frequency performance by dividing lead inductance among paths. Eight 400 Ω resistors in parallel = 50 Ω with 8× lower effective lead inductance.

N resistors in parallelReq = R / N

2-3. OIL COOLING

Mineral oil (transformer oil) dissipates approximately 2 W/cm² by natural convection. A 1-liter oil bath can continuously dissipate 100–200 W from a resistor array without exceeding 80 °C oil temperature.

CHAPTER 4 — ASSEMBLY PROCEDURES

  1. Arrange 10 resistors in a star pattern on copper groundplane. Each resistor from center hub to ground ring.
  2. Keep all leads <5 mm. Connect center hub to SO-239 center pin via very short, direct lead.
  3. Bond copper groundplane directly to heatsink. Use thermal compound and #6 screws.
  4. Bond SO-239 shell to heatsink (same ground potential).
  5. Verify DC resistance: 10 × 500 Ω in parallel = 50.0 Ω ±0.5%.

CHAPTER 5 — CALIBRATION PROCEDURE

  1. Measure DC resistance with 4-wire DMM. Record; target 50 ±1 Ω.
  2. Connect to NanoVNA or antenna analyzer. Measure |S11| from 1 to 500 MHz.
  3. Record SWR at 1, 7, 14, 50, 144, 440 MHz. All must be <1.5:1.
  4. Apply 10 W from QRP transceiver for 1 minute. Verify heatsink temperature <60 °C.
  5. Re-measure S11 after thermal soak; should be unchanged.

CHAPTER 6 — TUNING AND ADJUSTMENT

If SWR exceeds 1.2:1 above 100 MHz, add a small series compensation inductor or a shunt capacitor at the SO-239 center pin. Typical compensation: 1–2 nH series inductor (1 turn, 5 mm diameter air) at the SO-239 junction.
